CTEC English A: Forms 3–5 Completion Pathway

One structured route from Form 3 preparation to Form 5 completion, with term goals, learning evidence and teacher readiness reviews.

Babonneau Secondary School · Three-year plan

Your pathway at a glance

CTEC recognises achievement in separately assessed subject modules. This school plan groups learning into three years so students can see exactly what to learn, practise and review. Formal CTEC certification requires CXC assessments, not simply completing these website activities.

Form 3 · Module 1

Informative Discourse

Develop the reading, language and writing skills needed to explain information accurately.

Term 1 · Understand information

What to learn
  • Identify purpose, audience, main ideas and supporting details
  • Read school notices, informational articles, interviews, instructions and charts
  • Distinguish fact, opinion, inference and evidence
  • Review sentence structure, punctuation, subject–verb agreement and paragraph organisation
Student work: Annotate two short texts weekly; create a fact-and-opinion chart; write three organised informative paragraphs.
End-of-term checkpoint: Read an unfamiliar 250–350 word passage and answer 8 of 10 skill questions with evidence.

Term 2 · Organise and summarise

What to learn
  • Find key details across paragraphs and visual information
  • Practise paraphrasing and concise summaries
  • Select an appropriate register for a specific audience
  • Produce emails, notices, explanations and basic reports
Student work: Complete four summaries, two letters/emails and one report using a school or community context.
End-of-term checkpoint: Produce a coherent summary and informative text using a teacher checklist.

Term 3 · Apply and review

What to learn
  • Compare information from two sources and check reliability
  • Interpret tables, charts and graphics
  • Edit grammar, spelling, cohesion and tone
  • Complete timed comprehension and informative-writing tasks
Student work: Assemble an informative writing portfolio with drafts and revisions; complete two school-created practice assessments.
End-of-term checkpoint: Reach the school's suggested 75% mastery benchmark in two consecutive checks; teacher confirms readiness.
Form 3 end-of-year readiness review

Student submits corrected practice work, timed samples and an error log. The teacher checks understanding, writing or computation accuracy, independent work and performance across more than one assessment. A school benchmark is a preparation target, not an official CXC passing grade.

Form 4 · Module 2

Literary Discourse

Interpret literary texts closely and compose clear, engaging narratives.

Term 1 · Read with understanding

What to learn
  • Identify plot, setting, conflict, characterisation and theme
  • Interpret poetry, imagery, mood, tone and figurative language
  • Use direct textual evidence to support an explanation
  • Review dialogue punctuation and descriptive vocabulary
Student work: Keep a response journal for two poems and three short stories; write evidence-based paragraphs.
End-of-term checkpoint: Explain a writer's choice using a specific example from the text.

Term 2 · Write literary responses

What to learn
  • Summarise stories and literary extracts accurately
  • Compare character decisions, conflict and narrative viewpoint
  • Plan narratives with clear orientation, development and resolution
  • Use dialogue, description and paragraphing purposefully
Student work: Produce three short narratives with planning notes and revisions; submit two literary summaries.
End-of-term checkpoint: Create a coherent original story and explain the key features of an unseen extract.

Term 3 · Complete module preparation

What to learn
  • Practise poetry and prose comprehension
  • Revise narrative writing under timed conditions
  • Edit tense consistency, sentence fluency and vocabulary
  • Complete module-style school mock tasks
Student work: Submit a narrative-writing portfolio, a literary analysis sample and two practice papers.
End-of-term checkpoint: Teacher verifies the module checklist, writing quality and readiness for official assessment.
Form 4 end-of-year readiness review

Student submits corrected practice work, timed samples and an error log. The teacher checks understanding, writing or computation accuracy, independent work and performance across more than one assessment. A school benchmark is a preparation target, not an official CXC passing grade.

Form 5 · Module 3

Persuasive Discourse

Evaluate claims and evidence, then communicate convincing and responsible arguments.

Term 1 · Analyse persuasion

What to learn
  • Identify claim, reasons, evidence, bias and assumptions
  • Compare fact, opinion, exaggeration and emotional appeals
  • Interpret speeches, letters to editors, advertisements and infographics
  • Study counterarguments and respectful rebuttals
Student work: Annotate four persuasive texts and produce two claim–evidence maps.
End-of-term checkpoint: Evaluate the strength of evidence in an unseen persuasive text.

Term 2 · Compose arguments

What to learn
  • Write speeches, letters and argumentative essays for specific audiences
  • Structure introductions, claims, supporting paragraphs and conclusions
  • Integrate local examples without inventing facts
  • Revise tone, logical links, quotations and sentence accuracy
Student work: Draft and revise four persuasive pieces on school or Saint Lucia community issues.
End-of-term checkpoint: Present a sustained written argument with relevant reasons and a fair counterargument.

Term 3 · Final assessment and transition

What to learn
  • Practise timed persuasive comprehension and writing
  • Revisit informative and literary skills for overall proficiency
  • Complete required teacher-supervised assessment components
  • Review errors and plan any permitted module resit
Student work: Complete two full school mock cycles, final writing samples and the certification tracking sheet.
End-of-term checkpoint: Review official module results and completion requirements with the school's examination coordinator.
Form 5 end-of-year readiness review

Student submits corrected practice work, timed samples and an error log. The teacher checks understanding, writing or computation accuracy, independent work and performance across more than one assessment. A school benchmark is a preparation target, not an official CXC passing grade.

Weekly learning routine

Learn

Two 30–40 minute sessions reviewing a concept, model or worked example.

Practise

Two independent sessions completing mixed problems or comprehension and writing tasks.

Check and improve

One session correcting errors, retesting skills and recording one next target.

Recommended supplemental study time only. Teachers should adjust workload to students' support needs and the school timetable.

From school readiness to CTEC certification

  1. Review the latest CXC syllabus for all stated learning outcomes.
  2. Ask the school's examinations coordinator which modular sittings and entry routes are available for your cohort.
  3. Confirm permitted examination entry for your age/form and the proposed module sequence.
  4. Complete the required SBA or permitted alternative paper according to current CXC rules, including first-sitting obligations.
  5. Sit the required official modular papers and retain formal results.
  6. Check what each module result means and whether a resit is needed or allowed.
  7. After the last module, ask the examinations coordinator to verify the complete credential record with CXC.

Assessment policy alert: CXC lists SBA or alternative SBA arrangements for the 2027 CTEC English A and Mathematics offerings. CXC has also announced changes to SBA policy, so the school should recheck the entry rules every year.
